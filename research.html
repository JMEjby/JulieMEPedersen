Research

Political Polarisation & Agent-Based Modelling

My primary PhD research asks how population-level political polarisation can emerge from individual-level reasoning about values. I build agent-based models in which simulated agents infer the moral values of others from their behaviour and signals, and in turn modulate their own signalling accordingly. This work shows how rational, Bayesian inference under uncertainty — combined with a biased media landscape — can produce the kind of fractionalised, polarised distributions we observe empirically. I am interested in how the structure of the inference problem (which values are legible, how noisy signals are, how much agents update) shapes polarisation dynamics.

Moral Psychology & Moral Change

A second strand of my work concerns the mechanisms by which moral beliefs change over time. With collaborators, I am developing a framework called moralization by analogy, which proposes that moral concern can expand by mapping the morally relevant features of already-protected groups onto novel targets. I apply this framework to questions about when and why people extend moral consideration — for instance, to animals or to future generations. This work connects computational accounts of analogy and concept learning to questions in normative ethics and moral psychology.

Animal Ethics & Dietary Choice

I have investigated how psychological dissociation — the tendency to mentally separate animal-derived food products from the animals that produced them — moderates the role of disgust in reducing dairy consumption. This work sits at the intersection of moral psychology, consumer behaviour, and animal ethics, and contributes to understanding how people's moral concern for animals relates to (or fails to translate into) their food choices.

Applied Research at Rethink Priorities

Alongside my PhD, I work as a quantitative researcher at Rethink Priorities, an organisation conducting research to inform the priorities of effective altruists and allied funders. My applied work includes a completed agent-based modelling project, a messaging study on how different frames affect people's concern about AI risks, and data analysis projects written up for both internal clients and public audiences (including a post on the EA Forum).
